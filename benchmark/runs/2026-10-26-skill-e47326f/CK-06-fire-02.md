## Finding: "Users prefer the new purple colour scheme."

**Evidence strength: WEAK**
This claim should not be acted on. It is off-topic for the study, rests on a single offhand comment from one participant, and overstates a mild aesthetic remark as a population-level preference.

### Provenance
| Claim | Source | Observed or inferred | Traceable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Users" (plural, implying a pattern across participants) | No source — only INT-02 speaks to colour | Inferred | No — 1 of 6 participants is not a basis for "users" |
| "prefer" (comparative — new vs. something else) | INT-02 says "feels fresher," which implies a comparison but does not state a preference between designs | Inferred | No — "fresher" is not "prefer"; no prior/alternative was evaluated |
| "the new purple colour scheme" | INT-02: "I like the new purple accent colour" | Observed (partial) | Partial — participant said *accent colour*, not *colour scheme*. The finding inflates scope. |

The finding has three distinct problems in a single sentence: it generalises from n=1 to "users," upgrades "like … feels fresher" to "prefer," and expands "accent colour" to "colour scheme."

### Falsification pass
- **Strongest counter-hypothesis:** The other five participants either didn't notice the colour, didn't care, or disliked it — and none of them volunteered a comment because the study wasn't about colour. INT-02's remark is an incidental aside, not a signal of preference. With no data from INT-01, INT-03, INT-04, INT-05, or INT-06 on this topic, the null hypothesis (no detectable preference pattern) is at least as well-supported as the finding.
- **What would overturn this finding:** Direct, comparative preference data — e.g., a forced-choice question between old and new schemes across all six participants, or a dedicated preference study. Even confirmation that the other five participants were asked about colour and declined to comment would meaningfully change the picture.
- **Where the evidence underdetermines the claim:** Almost entirely. One unprompted positive remark about an accent colour from one participant in a study about filter button discoverability cannot support a preference claim about a colour scheme across users. This is not a close call.

### Pitfalls checked

| Pitfall | Triggered? | What triggered it |
|---|---|---|
| P-01 Headline Generality | Yes | "Users prefer the new purple colour scheme" is a label, not a behaviour. No trigger, no consequence, no observable action — it would read the same in a report about any product with a colour change. |
| P-02 Context Blindness | No | No prior research is referenced or implicated here; the problem is within-study, not cross-study. |
| P-03 Transcript Literalism | No | The issue is not over-trusting the transcript; the single data point is being correctly read, just massively over-extrapolated. |
| P-04 Volume Mistaken for Strength | Yes (inverted form) | The finding presents a population-level claim ("users prefer") on the basis of one unit from one source type. This is the extreme case of the pitfall: n=1 treated as a pattern. |
| P-05 Counter-Evidence Filed, Not Faced | No | There is no counter-evidence in the set — but equally, there is no corroborating evidence either. The absence of data from the other five participants is not acknowledged. |
| P-06 Formulaic Transfer Hedge | No | No transfer claim is made. |
| P-07 Trust Without Process | Yes | The finding is stated as a bare assertion with no reasoning connecting the evidence (one aside about an accent colour) to the conclusion (users prefer the colour scheme). The reader cannot audit how the claim was derived because no derivation is shown. |
| P-08 Narrowing Sold as Resolving | No | No causal or investigative claim about what the team should do next is made. |

A further structural issue worth naming, though it doesn't map cleanly to one pitfall: **the finding is off-topic for the study.** The study's sole stated question is filter button discoverability. Colour preference is not in scope, was not systematically probed, and should not appear as a finding at all — at most, INT-02's remark belongs in a "stray observations" or "out of scope" note for future consideration.

### What would change this verdict
Retracting the finding. There is no realistic amount of additional analysis of *this* evidence set that would support the claim as written — the data to support it was never collected, because the study wasn't designed to collect it. If colour preference matters to the team, it needs its own study.

### Tacit-knowledge prompt
Not applicable in the usual sense (the problem isn't ambiguity in a session; the problem is a finding manufactured from an aside). But one question for the researcher is worth asking: *Was colour discussed with any of the other five participants — even to the extent of them declining to comment — or is INT-02 the only session where it came up at all?* The answer determines whether this is "1 of 6 mentioned it positively" or "1 of 6 mentioned it, 5 were never asked," which are different (though both still insufficient) baselines.
