## Finding: "Listeners discover new podcasts mainly through friends, not in-app browsing."

**Evidence strength: WEAK**
The claim as written ("mainly") is a quantitative generalisation across the listener population, but the evidence provided is a single sentence from a single participant. Even granting that the other four sessions may corroborate it, nothing in what was supplied supports a "mainly" framing.

### Provenance
| Claim | Source | Observed or inferred | Traceable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listeners discover new podcasts through friends | INT-02: "I mostly find new shows because a friend sent me a link." | Observed (for INT-02 only) | Yes |
| Discovery happens **mainly** through friends (across the population) | Not cited | Inferred | No — no evidence provided for the quantitative generalisation |
| Discovery happens **not** through in-app browsing | Not cited | Inferred | No — nothing in the supplied evidence addresses in-app browsing at all, positively or negatively |

The second half of the claim ("not in-app browsing") is the more serious provenance gap: it's a negative claim with zero supporting evidence in what was shared. Absence of mention in one quote is not evidence of absence of behaviour.

### Falsification pass
- **Strongest counter-hypothesis:** INT-02's "mostly… because a friend sent me a link" describes one acquisition path for one recent show, not a stable discovery pattern. The same participant might also browse the in-app charts weekly without mentioning it, because the interview prompt pulled them toward the memorable story (the friend recommendation) rather than the habitual background behaviour (browsing). This is a known interview artefact — vivid single events out-recall routine behaviours.
- **What would overturn this finding:** Any of: (1) analytics showing in-app discovery surface usage is high among the same participants; (2) quotes from INT-01/03/04/05 describing in-app browsing as a primary path; (3) a direct question in the discussion guide that explicitly asked about in-app browsing and got consistent "no" answers across sessions. None of these are visible in what was shared.
- **Where the evidence underdetermines the claim:** Almost entirely. One quote cannot support a population-level "mainly," and no quote at all addresses the "not in-app browsing" half.

### What would change this verdict
Quotes or behavioural data from INT-01, INT-03, INT-04, and INT-05 addressing discovery channels — specifically whether in-app browsing was raised (prompted or unprompted) and what each participant said about it. If 4/5 or 5/5 sessions independently named friend-sharing as the primary channel *and* described in-app browsing as something they'd tried and abandoned, the finding would move from WEAK to UNCERTAIN-leaning-EMERGING. To reach STRONG, this would need corroboration from an independent source type (analytics on discovery-tab engagement, or a survey of a larger listener sample).

### Tacit-knowledge prompt
Across the five sessions, how many participants spontaneously mentioned in-app browsing as a discovery path, and how many were explicitly asked about it? The answer determines whether "not in-app browsing" is a finding from the data or an artefact of the discussion guide not probing for it.

## Summary for the user

The claim as stated is not supportable from the evidence provided. The two most serious issues:

1. **The negative half of the claim ("not in-app browsing") has no evidence behind it at all** in what was shared — it's an inference from silence, which isn't valid.
2. **The quantitative framing ("mainly") rests on one quote from one participant.** Even if that quote is representative, the synthesis hasn't shown the work.

Before acting on this finding to inform the discovery-tab redesign decision, I'd want to see the evidence from the other four sessions and a clear account of what the discussion guide actually asked about in-app browsing. If that evidence exists and corroborates, this becomes a reasonable EMERGING finding; if it doesn't, the current claim should be withdrawn and restated as something narrower like *"At least one participant (INT-02) described friend-sharing as their primary discovery path; the role of in-app browsing was not established in this study."*
