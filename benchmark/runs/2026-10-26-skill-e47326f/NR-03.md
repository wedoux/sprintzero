## Finding: "The streak mechanic has shifted from a learning aid to a loss-aversion habit for long-streak users."

**Evidence strength: WEAK**
On the material provided, this reads as a plausible hypothesis dressed as a conclusion — one participant's self-report is being generalised into a behavioural shift across a segment, and the "shifted from X to Y" framing claims a temporal change the study design cannot see.

### Falsification pass
- **Strongest counter-hypothesis:** The streak mechanic was *always* primarily a habit/loss-aversion mechanic for these users, and INT-03 is simply the first participant articulate enough to name it. In that case, nothing has "shifted" — the research has just surfaced what the mechanic was doing all along. This matters because the product implication is different: "stop something that stopped working" vs. "the mechanic never did the thing we thought it did."
- **What would overturn this finding:** (a) longitudinal data or retrospective accounts from the other five participants showing their relationship to the streak genuinely changed over time; (b) evidence from the other five sessions that most of them still experience the streak as a learning aid, which would reduce this to an INT-03-specific pattern.
- **Where the evidence underdetermines the claim:** The study is cross-sectional (one session per user) but the claim is temporal ("shifted"). No design that only observes users at one point in time can establish a shift without retrospective self-report, and none is cited. Also: five of six sessions are invisible in the material provided, so the "long-streak users" generalisation is underdetermined by what's shown.

### What would change this verdict
Evidence from the other five sessions — specifically, whether participants other than INT-03 describe (a) a current loss-aversion relationship with the streak, and (b) a retrospective sense that this is different from how they used to relate to it. If 4–5 of 6 corroborate both halves, the verdict moves toward UNCERTAIN-leaning-STRONG (still single-source-type, but internally consistent). If only INT-03 says it, the finding should be rewritten as "INT-03 describes a loss-aversion relationship with the streak; worth probing in follow-up research" — a hypothesis, not a conclusion.

### Tacit-knowledge prompt
INT-03 said "at this point," which implies they perceive a change in their own relationship to the streak. Was there a moment in that session — a pause, a tone shift, a follow-up answer — that would help distinguish between "I used to learn but now I just maintain the streak" (which supports the finding) and "I've always mostly been here for the streak, I just noticed it today" (which doesn't)? And separately: across the other five sessions, did any participant volunteer something similar unprompted, or did it only come up when asked?
