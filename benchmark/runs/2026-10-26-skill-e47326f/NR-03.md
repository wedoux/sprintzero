## Finding: "One long-streak participant said they now open the app mainly to avoid breaking the streak, not because they feel they're learning much."

**Evidence strength: STRONG**
The claim is a careful, scoped restatement of what one participant said — it does not generalise, does not infer motive beyond what was stated, and matches the quoted evidence almost verbatim.

### Provenance
| Claim | Source | Observed or inferred | Traceable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| "One long-streak participant" | INT-03 is one participant; study recruited only users with 7+ day streaks | Observed | Yes |
| "said they now open the app mainly to avoid breaking the streak" | INT-03: "I open the app mostly to not break the streak at this point" | Observed | Yes |
| "not because they feel they're learning much" | INT-03: "not because I'm learning much" | Observed | Yes |

Every clause in the claim traces directly to the quoted utterance from INT-03. The qualifier "one...participant" correctly bounds the scope to a single speaker.

### Falsification pass
- **Strongest counter-hypothesis:** INT-03's self-report may not reflect their actual motivation — people routinely misreport why they do things, especially in moderated sessions where "I'm doing this for a shallow reason" can be a performative or self-deprecating framing. But the claim as written only asserts that the participant *said* this, not that it's true, so this counter-hypothesis doesn't actually threaten the claim.
- **What would overturn this finding:** A transcript showing INT-03 did not say this, or said it with qualifiers the summary strips out (e.g. "sometimes I feel like I open the app just for the streak, though mostly I'm still learning"). Without the fuller transcript context, this remains the one real exposure.
- **Where the evidence underdetermines the claim:** The claim is appropriately narrow and matches the evidence. It does *not* say anything about what this means for the streak mechanic overall, how common this experience is, or whether the streak is a problem — all of which would be underdetermined. The reviewer should watch that downstream synthesis doesn't quietly promote this single quote into a thematic finding.

### What would change this verdict
Access to the five other session transcripts. If INT-03's framing is echoed by other long-streak participants, this quote becomes the leading edge of a pattern worth naming. If INT-03 is isolated and the other five describe the streak as motivating in a healthy way, this quote is a single data point that should be treated as such — still valid, but the study record should note the asymmetry when this quote gets cited later.

Separately: the study's declared sample limit (streak-7+ only) means this finding, even if replicated across all six sessions, cannot speak to lapsed or new users. That limit is correctly declared and this claim respects it, but any downstream use of the quote should carry the same scope.

### Tacit-knowledge prompt
INT-03 said "mostly" and "at this point" — both are hedges that suggest a shift over time rather than a steady state. Was there a moment earlier in the session where INT-03 described the streak as useful or motivating, such that this quote represents a change of heart rather than a baseline attitude? That distinction matters for how the quote should be framed if it gets cited in a decision document.
