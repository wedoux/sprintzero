## Finding: "3 of 8 participants preferred the subscription model over in-app purchases."

**Evidence strength: WEAK**
The literal count is defensible, but the framing turns absence of mention into absence of preference, and three unprompted mentions into a majority-worthy signal. As stated, this finding would mislead a pricing decision.

### Provenance
| Claim | Source | Observed or inferred | Traceable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| INT-01 preferred subscription over in-app purchases | "I'd pay a flat monthly fee if it meant no ads." | Inferred | Partial — participant expressed willingness to pay a subscription *to avoid ads*, not a preference over in-app purchases specifically. The comparison in the finding isn't the comparison the participant made. |
| INT-02 preferred subscription over in-app purchases | "Subscription makes sense to me, I already do it for two other apps." | Inferred | Partial — participant endorsed subscriptions in general; no explicit comparison to in-app purchases. |
| INT-03 preferred subscription over in-app purchases | "I'd rather subscribe than deal with in-app purchases." | Observed | Yes — this is the only one of the three that directly makes the stated comparison. |
| "3 of 8 participants" as a meaningful ratio | INT-04 through INT-08 silent | Inferred | No — silence is being treated as "did not prefer subscription," which the evidence does not support. The denominator is doing work the data doesn't justify. |

### Falsification pass
- **Strongest counter-hypothesis:** The three mentions reflect what came up unprompted in open-ended conversation, not a measured preference distribution. If participants weren't asked directly about subscription vs. in-app purchases, then 5 of 8 being silent tells us nothing about their preferences — only that the topic didn't surface. The finding as written implies a 3/8 vs. 5/8 split; the evidence only supports "3 participants raised this spontaneously, 5 didn't mention it either way."
- **What would overturn this finding:** Confirmation that INT-04 through INT-08 were asked the comparison question directly and declined to express a preference (which would make the 3/8 ratio meaningful), *or* evidence that they were never asked (which would make the ratio meaningless and the finding need rewriting).
- **Where the evidence underdetermines the claim:** Three distinct gaps. (1) Whether the five silent participants were asked. (2) Whether INT-01 and INT-02 actually prefer subscription *over in-app purchases* specifically, versus liking subscriptions in general or disliking ads specifically. (3) Whether the discussion guide prompted this comparison consistently across sessions.

### What would change this verdict
Confirmation of what INT-04 through INT-08 were actually asked. If the discussion guide included a direct subscription-vs-IAP question and five participants gave no preference, the finding could be rewritten as "3 of 8 expressed a preference for subscription when asked; 5 were neutral or undecided" — still a weak signal from eight interviews, but at least an honest one. If the question wasn't consistently asked, the finding should be rewritten as "3 participants spontaneously raised a preference for subscription; the comparison was not systematically probed," and the "3 of 8" framing dropped entirely.
