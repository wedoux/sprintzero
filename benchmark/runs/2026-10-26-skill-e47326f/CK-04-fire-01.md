## Finding: "The new onboarding carousel works."

**Evidence strength: WEAK**
There is nothing here to evaluate. The claim is a bare assertion with no evidence, no definition of "works," and no study record behind it — this should not be acted on in its current form.

### Provenance

| Claim | Source | Observed or inferred | Traceable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| "The new onboarding carousel works." | Not cited — no evidence provided | Neither (asserted, not derived) | No — there is no supporting evidence, no study, and no belief register entry behind this statement |

Every substantive element of this claim is unsupported:
- **"New"** — no baseline or prior version is referenced.
- **"Onboarding carousel"** — the artefact is named but not described (how many steps, what content, which users see it).
- **"Works"** — no success criterion is defined (completion rate? activation? retention? user-reported comprehension?).

### Falsification pass
- **Strongest counter-hypothesis:** The carousel does *not* work by any meaningful product metric — for example, completion rates may be high (users tap through) while activation or D7 retention is flat or worse, which is a common pattern for onboarding flows that users dismiss rather than engage with. Without a defined success metric, "high tap-through" and "works" are being conflated.
- **What would overturn this finding:** A pre-registered success metric (e.g. "≥60% of new users complete the first core action within 24h") plus a comparison against either a baseline cohort or a control arm, with the result stated.
- **Where the evidence underdetermines the claim:** Everywhere. There is no evidence base at all — not thin evidence, not ambiguous evidence, no evidence. The claim is underdetermined in full.

|---|---|---|
| P-01 Headline Generality | **Yes** | "Works" is the textbook label-not-behaviour failure. No observable behaviour, no trigger, no consequence. The sentence would read identically for any feature on any product — swap "onboarding carousel" for "checkout flow" or "search bar" and nothing changes. |
| P-02 Context Blindness | **Yes** (by omission) | No belief register entry, no prior study record. Either prior research on onboarding exists and is being ignored, or it doesn't exist and that should be stated. Silently treating this as a fresh, uncontested claim is itself the pitfall. |

Even in the best case where some supporting data existed, "works" would almost certainly overstate what the data could support (e.g. "users complete the carousel" ≠ "the carousel achieves the onboarding goal"). |

### What would change this verdict
A single document stating: (1) what "works" was defined to mean before the data was collected, (2) what was measured, against what baseline or control, over what period, for which users, and (3) the result. Without those three things, there is no finding here to QA — only a stakeholder-friendly sentence.

### Tacit-knowledge prompt
Not applicable — there is no researcher-run session or study to draw tacit knowledge from. The prior question is whether any research was done at all. If the answer is "we're going on team impressions / stakeholder feedback / early metrics that look encouraging," that should be stated plainly rather than compressed into the word "works."

---

**Meta-note on this QA pass:** This is the degenerate case the skill is built to catch. The claim arrived with no belief register, no study record, and no evidence — three empty inputs. A less rigorous review might treat the absence of evidence as a reason to withhold judgment ("can't evaluate without more info"). The correct call is the opposite: a confident-sounding claim with zero provenance is a **WEAK** verdict, not an **UNCERTAIN** one. Uncertain means the signal exists but can't yet be adjudicated; weak means the claim as stated does not clear the bar to be acted on. This one doesn't.
