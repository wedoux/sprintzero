## Finding: "4 of 5 participants completed checkout without help."

**Evidence strength:** UNCERTAIN
Five sessions is a thin base — the count is accurate for who was observed, but it's too small to say what proportion of users generally will complete checkout unaided.

**Claim matches its evidence:** Yes — the claim is scoped to the five participants observed, not generalised to "users".

### Is it true for these people?

**Falsification pass**
- **Strongest counter-hypothesis:** The one participant who needed help may represent a larger segment (first-time users, a particular device, a specific payment method) that happens to be underrepresented at 1-in-5. "Without help" also depends on how help was defined in-session — did the moderator intervene, or only count explicit requests?
- **What would overturn this finding:** Session recordings or notes showing that moderators prompted or hinted during checkout in the four "successful" sessions, which would mean completion wasn't actually unaided.
- **Where the evidence underdetermines the claim:** Five sessions can't tell you a completion rate for any population beyond those five. The claim doesn't attempt that, but a reader should not infer "80% of users complete checkout" from it.

**Counter-evidence:** None cited in the material provided.

**Unsupported claims:** None — the single factual claim traces to INT-01 through INT-05.

### Tacit-knowledge prompt
For the one participant who did not complete checkout unaided: what kind of help was given, and at what point in the flow? And in the four "successful" sessions, did the moderator ever prompt, hint or clarify — even briefly — during checkout? The definition of "without help" is doing a lot of work in this claim.

### Decision boundary
- **Supports:** In this specific sample of five observed sessions, four participants completed checkout without needing help.
- **Doesn't support:** Any claim about a completion rate for users generally, for any specific segment, or for any device, payment method or context not represented in these five sessions. Also does not support a product decision that depends on knowing *why* the one participant struggled.
- **What would close the gap:** A larger sample with recorded failure modes — enough sessions, or complementary analytics on checkout drop-off, to tell whether the 1-in-5 failure represents an edge case or a pattern.
