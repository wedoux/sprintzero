## Finding: "Users prefer the new purple colour scheme."

**Evidence strength:** WEAK
One participant made a passing positive comment about the colour. That is one voice, on a topic the study wasn't asking about, with no comparison to any alternative.

**Claim matches its evidence:** No — one person saying a colour "feels fresher" does not support a claim about what "users prefer", and no comparison was made.

### Is it true for these people?

**Falsification pass**
- **Strongest counter-hypothesis:** INT-02 was being polite or making small talk about a visual detail they noticed; the comment reflects novelty ("fresher") rather than preference. The other five participants said nothing about colour at all, which is at least as consistent with indifference as with agreement.
- **What would overturn this finding:** A direct comparison task (old vs. new scheme) with explicit preference questions, across a sample large enough to show a pattern. Even one participant saying they disliked the purple would seriously weaken it.
- **Where the evidence underdetermines the claim:** Five of six participants gave no view on the colour. Nothing was asked about preference. There is no "old scheme" baseline in the evidence. The claim is almost entirely underdetermined.

**Counter-evidence:** The silence of INT-01, INT-03, INT-04, INT-05 and INT-06 on the colour is not counter-evidence in the strict sense, but it means the claim rests on 1 of 6 participants — and that one made an aesthetic remark, not a preference statement.

**Unsupported claims:** The entire claim is unsupported as worded. The word "prefer" implies a comparison that was never made. The word "users" implies a pattern that one comment cannot establish.

### Decision boundary
- **Supports:** One participant, unprompted or otherwise, found the new purple accent colour fresh. That's it.
- **Doesn't support:** Any claim about what "users" prefer. Any comparison between the old and new schemes. Any decision to keep, change, or expand the purple scheme on preference grounds.
- **What would close the gap:** A direct preference comparison — old scheme vs. new scheme — with explicit questions, across a sample designed to show preference rather than discoverability. Alternatively: drop the claim entirely, since the study wasn't designed to answer this question.

**Safe to act on:** Not yet — the claim says far more than its evidence allows (CK-05 fires, P-01 and P-07 trigger), and it answers a question the study didn't ask (CK-06 fires). Two further checks (CK-01, CK-02) could not run because the belief register and study limits were not declared.
