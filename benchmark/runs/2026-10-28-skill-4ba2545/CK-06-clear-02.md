## Finding: "Unexpected finding (not asked): the moderator noticed participants pausing around the five-minute mark; worth tracking in future sessions."

**Evidence strength:** WEAK
A single moderator observation from one session, labelled honestly as unexpected and framed only as something to watch in future rounds.

**Claim matches its evidence:** Yes — the claim is explicitly flagged as "worth tracking", not as a finding to act on, and stays within what one observation from one moderator can support.

### Is it true for these people?

**Falsification pass**
- **Strongest counter-hypothesis:** The pause pattern is an artefact of session design (session length, task structure, moderator fatigue affecting what gets noticed) rather than a real behavioural rhythm. INT-05's note that "sessions were running long" is itself a clue that the moderator's attention may have been shaped by session management, not participant behaviour.
- **What would overturn this finding:** Timestamped behavioural data across the six sessions showing no clustering of pauses around the five-minute mark; or moderator notes from INT-01 to INT-04 and INT-06 explicitly recording no such pattern.
- **Where the evidence underdetermines the claim:** Only one moderator note is cited. There's no indication whether the other five sessions showed the same pattern, whether "most people" means 2 of 6 or 5 of 6, or what "paused" meant operationally.

**Counter-evidence:** None cited in the material.

**Unsupported claims:** None — the claim is narrowly framed as a moderator observation worth tracking, which is what the evidence supports.

### Tacit-knowledge prompt
The moderator in INT-05 said sessions were running long and that "most people" paused around the five-minute mark. Two things would help resolve what this is: (1) In how many of the six sessions did you see this pause, and roughly how many participants within each? (2) Was the five-minute mark correlated with a task boundary, a moderator intervention, or something the participant was doing on screen?

### Decision boundary
- **Supports:** Flagging a pattern worth watching in future rounds.
- **Doesn't support:** Any claim that participants systematically pause at five minutes, that the pause means anything about the filter button or the product, or that session design should change on the basis of it.
- **What would close the gap:** Timestamped behavioural data (or structured moderator notes) across all six sessions showing whether the pause pattern replicates and what, if anything, it coincides with on screen.

**Safe to act on:** Yes — but only for the action the claim actually proposes, which is "track in future sessions". The claim is honest about its evidence and does not ask anyone to change the product. If this observation gets repeated later as justification for a design change or a behavioural claim about users, that would be a different claim and would need different evidence.
