## Finding: "In this sample, one participant said they discover new podcasts mainly through a friend's link, rather than in-app browsing."

**Evidence strength:** WEAK
A single participant's statement from one of five sessions. It's a real signal but a thin one — one person is a starting point, not a pattern.

**Claim matches its evidence:** Yes — the claim is explicitly scoped to "in this sample" and "one participant", and attributes the behaviour to that participant alone rather than generalising to listeners at large.

### Is it true for these people?

**Falsification pass**
- **Strongest counter-hypothesis:** The other four participants may well have described in-app browsing or other discovery routes; one person's experience tells us nothing about whether friend-sharing is typical or unusual in this sample. The claim avoids this trap by not generalising, but a reader could still over-weight the single data point just because it's the one surfaced.
- **What would overturn this finding:** INT-02's full transcript showing the quote was about a one-off case ("that one show") rather than their main discovery habit, or a clarification from the researcher that "mostly" referred to a specific recent period.
- **Where the evidence underdetermines the claim:** The word "mainly" in the claim rests on a single quoted sentence. We don't see whether INT-02 was asked follow-up questions about frequency, or whether other discovery routes came up elsewhere in their session.

**Counter-evidence:** None cited in the material provided. The other four sessions (INT-01, INT-03, INT-04, INT-05) are referenced as existing but no excerpts are included, so we can't tell whether they corroborate or contradict.

**Unsupported claims:** None — the single claim traces directly to the INT-02 quote.

### Tacit-knowledge prompt (optional — ask only if a researcher who ran the sessions is available)
Did the other four participants (INT-01, INT-03, INT-04, INT-05) describe their discovery routes? If so, how does INT-02 sit against them — is friend-sharing a shared pattern, a minority route, or an outlier? And when INT-02 said "mostly", did they describe other routes they also use, or was the friend-link route presented as the whole picture?

### Decision boundary
- **Supports:** One participant in a five-session study said friend-sharing is their main route to new podcasts. That's a real data point and a reason to ask the question more deliberately in future sessions.
- **Doesn't support:** Any claim about how listeners in general discover podcasts, how common friend-sharing is in this sample, or whether in-app browsing is or isn't working. Even within this study, we don't know what the other four participants said.
- **What would close the gap:** The equivalent one-line summary from each of the other four sessions — did they describe friend-sharing, in-app browsing, charts, recommendations, something else? Five short answers would turn one quote into a readable pattern (or a clearly scattered one).

**Safe to act on:** Not yet — the claim is honest about its scope, but one participant from five is too thin to drive a decision about redesigning the discovery tab. Use it as a question to ask the other sessions, not as a finding.
