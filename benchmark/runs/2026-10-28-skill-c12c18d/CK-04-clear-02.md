## Finding: "Cutting the onboarding carousel from five screens to two is the right call."

**Evidence strength:** WEAK
No evidence was cited. The claim is a product recommendation resting on a team principle, not on anything observed from users.

**Claim matches its evidence:** No — the claim prescribes a specific product change ("five to two") that no cited evidence or declared belief actually specifies; B-01 names a time target, not a screen count.

### Is it true for these people?

**Falsification pass**
- **Strongest counter-hypothesis:** Two screens may fail to orient returning-category users who rely on a specific carousel screen (e.g. permissions, account linking) to complete setup. Fewer screens could lengthen total onboarding time if each remaining screen gets denser.
- **What would overturn this finding:** Session data or usability sessions showing that returning-category users complete onboarding faster, with equal or better comprehension, at two screens than at five — or showing that specific carousel screens carry information users act on later.
- **Where the evidence underdetermines the claim:** Entirely. Nothing is cited about what users do on the five screens today, which screens they skip or dwell on, or whether screen count is what drives time-to-complete.

**Counter-evidence:** None presented, which is itself a gap — a five-to-two cut should at minimum engage with what the three removed screens were doing.

**Unsupported claims:** The entire claim. There is no cited evidence that (a) current onboarding exceeds the 60-second target, (b) screen count is the lever, or (c) two is the right number rather than three or four.

### Decision boundary
- **Supports:** The team has a shared principle (B-01) that returning-category onboarding should take under 60 seconds. That principle justifies *investigating* whether current onboarding meets the target.
- **Doesn't support:** That current onboarding fails the target; that screen count is the bottleneck; that two screens is the right destination; or that this change is "right" in any evaluable sense.
- **What would close the gap:** Timed observation or analytics on current onboarding for returning-category users, showing (a) where in the five screens time is actually spent, and (b) which screens carry information users act on later. Without this, any screen-count decision is a guess dressed as a conclusion.
