## Finding: "Hikers want maps that work offline — stakeholders confirmed it in last week's review — so offline download will be on by default."

**Evidence strength:** WEAK
The claim rests on one person's personal experience (rated low confidence by the holder, and actively disputed by the ops lead) plus stakeholder agreement in a review. No evidence from hikers is cited.

**Claim matches its evidence:** No — the claim is stated as a settled user need and a product decision, but the only support is one low-confidence personal belief and attributed stakeholder opinion. Multiple checks fire (see below).

### Is it true for these people?

**Falsification pass**
- **Strongest counter-hypothesis:** Coverage is adequate on the trails the product actually serves (as the ops lead argues in the dissent note on B-01), and the offline-map belief is a generalisation from two personal experiences on unrepresentative trails. Hikers may want offline maps for reasons other than signal loss (battery, data cost, international travel), or may not prioritise it at all — we have no data either way.
- **What would overturn this finding:** Evidence from hikers themselves — interviews, support tickets, reviews, or usage data — showing either that signal loss isn't a common pain, or that offline maps aren't a priority relative to other needs. Also: coverage data on the trails the product serves, which the ops lead implies exists.
- **Where the evidence underdetermines the claim:** The evidence base is empty on the people-side. There is no hiker in this material — only an internal belief and internal agreement.

**Counter-evidence:** The dissent note on B-01 — the ops lead says coverage on top trails is fine — directly contradicts the premise of the claim. The synthesis does not address this; it treats stakeholder agreement as settling the question, when in fact the register already records internal disagreement.

**Unsupported claims:** All three parts of the claim are unsupported by participant evidence: (1) that hikers want offline maps, (2) the implied reason (signal loss), and (3) that defaulting download to on is the right response.

### Decision boundary
- **Supports:** That one team member believes, from personal experience on two trails, that signal loss is a problem; and that stakeholders in a review did not object to adding offline maps. That is the entire supported scope.
- **Doesn't support:** Any claim about what hikers in general want; that signal loss is the reason they'd want offline maps; that default-on is the right default (as opposed to opt-in, on-demand, or route-specific download); or that this should be prioritised over whatever else is on the roadmap.
- **What would close the gap:** Evidence from actual hikers. The single most useful next step: a handful of interviews or a review of existing support tickets / app-store reviews mentioning signal, maps, or offline use. Even a quick look would tell you whether this is a real pain or an internal projection — and would also test the ops lead's disagreement.

**Safe to act on:** Not yet — the claim says much more than its evidence allows, rests on attributed agreement rather than hiker evidence, and contradicts an unresolved internal disagreement already recorded in the belief register.
