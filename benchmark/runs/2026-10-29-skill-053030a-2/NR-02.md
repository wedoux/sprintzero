## Finding: "In this sample, one participant said they discover new podcasts mainly through a friend's link, rather than in-app browsing."

**Evidence strength:** WEAK
A single participant quote, honestly scoped to "one participant" and "in this sample." The signal is real but thin.

**Claim matches its evidence:** Yes — the claim says exactly what INT-02 said, scoped to one person in this sample, with no generalisation to "users" or "listeners."

### Is it true for these people?

**Falsification pass**
- **Strongest counter-hypothesis:** INT-02 may use in-app browsing frequently too, and simply named the friend's link as the most recent or memorable instance. "Mainly" is INT-02's own word, but a single-sentence quote doesn't rule out other discovery routes the same participant uses.
- **What would overturn this finding:** A later moment in the INT-02 session where the participant describes regular in-app browsing as their primary route, which would reframe the quoted sentence as context rather than their main pattern.
- **Where the evidence underdetermines the claim:** One quote from one participant underdetermines any pattern claim. The finding stays safe only because it is scoped to "one participant" — it does not claim a pattern.

**Counter-evidence:** none in the supplied material.

**Unsupported claims:** none — the single claim traces directly to the INT-02 quote.

### Decision boundary
- **Supports:** One participant in this sample described friend-shared links as their main route to new podcasts.
- **Doesn't support:** Any claim about how listeners in general, or even other participants in this study, find new podcasts; any claim that in-app browsing is or isn't working; any decision about redesigning the discovery tab.
- **What would close the gap:** The same question put to the other four participants (INT-01, INT-03, INT-04, INT-05) — if a majority describe out-of-app routes (friends, social, newsletters) as primary, the discovery tab's role itself becomes the question worth investigating.

**Safe to act on:** Not yet — the claim is honest, but one quote from one participant is too thin to inform a redesign decision, and four other sessions in the same study have not been reported on.
