## Finding: "Users will keep up their watering streaks for months once the feature ships."

**Evidence strength:** WEAK
One in-session reaction from one participant is not evidence of months-long behaviour. The claim is far ahead of what a single 15-minute unmoderated session can show.

**Claim matches its evidence:** No — the claim projects multi-month behaviour from a single in-the-moment reaction by one participant; CK-02, CK-03, CK-05 and P-01, P-04 all fire on this.

### Is it true for these people?

**Falsification pass**
- **Strongest counter-hypothesis:** INT-03 is reacting to novelty. People often say "I'd keep that going" when first shown a streak mechanic and abandon it within days. The quote is a stated intention in the moment, not an observed sustained behaviour.
- **What would overturn this finding:** Longitudinal data — e.g. a 4-week or 12-week diary study, or shipped-feature analytics showing streak retention curves over months. Even a two-week follow-up with INT-03 would do more than the current evidence.
- **Where the evidence underdetermines the claim:** Completely. One quote from one of eight participants, captured in a 15-minute session, cannot support any claim about multi-month behaviour. The other seven participants aren't cited at all.

**Counter-evidence:** None cited in the material — but the absence of any contradicting voices from the other seven participants is itself a gap, not a confirmation.

**Unsupported claims:**
- "Users" (plural, general) — only one participant is quoted.
- "for months" — no evidence of any duration beyond the 15-minute session.
- "will keep up" (as prediction) — the quote is a stated preference, not observed persistence.

### Tacit-knowledge prompt
If the researcher who ran INT-03 is available: did the "I'd keep that going" land as genuine enthusiasm, or as the kind of polite agreement unmoderated participants often give to a novel-looking feature? Was there any hesitation, qualifier, or body-language signal in the recording? This won't rescue the claim, but it may change how much weight to give the one quote the claim rests on.

### Decision boundary
- **Supports:** One participant, shown a streak counter in a 15-minute unmoderated session, reacted positively to the concept.
- **Doesn't support:** Any claim about what the other seven participants think; any claim about behaviour beyond the session itself; any claim about multi-month retention; any claim about "users" as a group.
- **What would close the gap:** A longitudinal study — the method limit explicitly flags this. Even a 2–4 week diary study with the same eight participants, or post-launch retention analytics once the feature ships to a small cohort, would move this from unsupported projection to testable claim.

**Safe to act on:** Not yet — the claim violates the study's own declared method limit, generalises from one participant to all users, and projects months of behaviour from a 15-minute session. The underlying signal (one person liked the concept) is real but very small; it supports further investigation, not a shipping decision.
