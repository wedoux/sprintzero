**Evidence strength:** WEAK
One participant quote, honestly attributed as one participant. A real signal worth noting, but a single data point.

**Claim matches its evidence:** Yes — the claim says "one participant", quotes them accurately, and makes no generalisation beyond them.

### Is it true for these people?

**Falsification pass**
- **Strongest counter-hypothesis:** INT-03's self-report may not match their behaviour — people often narrate their habits in self-deprecating ways ("I'm just doing it for the streak") while still absorbing more than they credit. The quote describes a feeling about learning, not a measurement of it.
- **What would overturn this finding:** Evidence from INT-03 themselves — e.g. in-session performance, retention data, or a direct contradiction later in the same interview — showing active engagement with content rather than reflex opening.
- **Where the evidence underdetermines the claim:** The claim is narrow (one person said this), so it's not overreaching. But it tells us nothing about whether this feeling is common even among long-streak users — the other five sessions aren't referenced.

**Counter-evidence:** None cited in the material provided. Whether INT-01, 02, 04, 05, 06 said anything that contradicts or complicates this isn't visible here — worth checking before this quote travels further.

**Unsupported claims:** None. The claim is a direct, attributed restatement of the quote.

### Tacit-knowledge prompt

### Decision boundary
- **Supports:** One long-streak user, in their own words, framed streak-maintenance as the main driver of app opens and distinguished that from learning.
- **Doesn't support:** Any claim about how common this feeling is, even among long-streak users; any claim about lapsed or new users (explicitly ruled out by the sample limit); any claim about whether the streak mechanic should be kept, changed or removed.
- **What would close the gap:** A targeted look at the other five sessions — did anyone else, prompted or unprompted, describe the streak and the learning as separable? Even one or two more voices would move this from "one person said something striking" to "a pattern worth investigating". If nothing like it appears, that's informative too.
