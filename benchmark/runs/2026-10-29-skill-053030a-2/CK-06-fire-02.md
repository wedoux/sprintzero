## Finding: "Users prefer the new purple colour scheme."

**Evidence strength:** WEAK
One participant made a passing positive remark about a purple accent colour. That is not evidence of preference, and it is not evidence about "users" in general.

**Claim matches its evidence:** No — one person's offhand comment about an "accent colour" is being reported as a shared preference for a "colour scheme".

### Is it true for these people?

**Falsification pass**
- **Strongest counter-hypothesis:** INT-02 was being polite or making small talk; the comment reflects momentary impression, not preference. The other five participants said nothing about colour at all, which under a preference claim is the dog that didn't bark.
- **What would overturn this finding:** Any of the other five participants expressing indifference or dislike for the purple; or INT-02 themselves, asked directly, saying they have no real preference between schemes.
- **Where the evidence underdetermines the claim:** Almost entirely. One unprompted positive remark from one participant cannot establish preference, cannot establish it across users, and cannot establish it about a "scheme" when only an "accent colour" was mentioned.

**Counter-evidence:** The silence of INT-01, INT-03, INT-04, INT-05 and INT-06 on colour is not counter-evidence to a specific claim, but it is counter-evidence to a general one. Five of six participants gave the colour scheme no reaction worth recording.

**Unsupported claims:** The words "users" (plural, general), "prefer" (comparative judgment), and "colour scheme" (whole visual system) all go beyond what INT-02 said. The entire claim as worded is unsupported; only "one participant said they liked the purple accent colour" is supported.

### Decision boundary
- **Supports:** One participant, in one session, made an unprompted positive remark about the new purple accent colour.
- **Doesn't support:** Any claim about user preference, about the colour scheme as a whole, or about how the redesign is received more generally. Also doesn't support anything about Q-01 (filter button discoverability), which is what the study was commissioned to answer.
- **What would close the gap:** A study actually designed to ask about aesthetic preference — direct questions to all participants, ideally with the old scheme shown as a comparison. The current study wasn't built to answer this question and cannot be stretched to.

**Safe to act on:** Not yet — the claim overreaches its evidence by an enormous margin and answers no question the study asked. If the team wants to know about colour preference, that needs its own study.
