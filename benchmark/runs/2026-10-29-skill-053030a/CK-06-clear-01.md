## Finding: "5 of 6 participants did not notice the new filter button without being prompted."

**Evidence strength:** STRONG
In this sample of six, the pattern is near-unanimous and the evidence is consistent: five of six participants describe not noticing the filter button unprompted, and the sixth is clearly identified as the exception.

**Claim matches its evidence:** Yes — the claim states a count ("5 of 6 participants") rather than generalising to "users", and the quoted sessions directly support each half of it.

### Is it true for these people?

**Falsification pass**
- **Strongest counter-hypothesis:** The moderated remote setup itself may have shaped attention — participants aware they're being observed may scan differently than they would in normal use, in either direction. The claim is about what happened in these sessions, so this doesn't overturn it, but it bears on any later inference about ordinary use.
- **What would overturn this finding:** Session recordings showing that participants INT-01 through INT-05 did in fact look at or interact with the filter button before claiming not to have seen it (self-report contradicted by behaviour). None supplied here — the evidence is self-report only.
- **Where the evidence underdetermines the claim:** The claim itself is tightly scoped to the count, and the evidence supports it directly. The underdetermination lies in what follows from it, not in the claim as written.

**Counter-evidence:** INT-06 saw the button immediately. The claim already accommodates this by saying "5 of 6", not "all". No counter-evidence is being filed-and-ignored.

**Unsupported claims:** none — every element of the claim traces to a specific session.

### Decision boundary
- **Supports:** In this sample of six moderated sessions, the filter button was not noticed without prompting by nearly all participants. That is sufficient signal to treat discoverability as a real problem worth addressing.
- **Doesn't support:** Any claim about the broader user base, about how this would play out in unmoderated use, or about which redesign would fix it. The study did not test alternatives.
- **What would close the gap:** If the decision is only "is there a discoverability problem worth acting on?", the gap is already closed for a sample this small and consistent. If the decision is "which redesign works?", the next piece of evidence is a comparative test of candidate placements or treatments — not more of the same study.

**Safe to act on:** Yes — the claim is tightly scoped, the evidence is direct, and five of six is a strong signal in a six-person sample. One caveat: with no limits declared, the readout can't audit what the study design rules out, so any decision beyond "there is a discoverability problem here" should be made with that in mind.
